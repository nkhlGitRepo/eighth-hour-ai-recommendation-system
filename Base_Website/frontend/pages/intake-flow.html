What makes a photo work

  • Your whole body in frame, head to feet, gives the best estimate. A photo that stops just above your feet still works.
  • Face the camera, standing straight — not turned or leaning.
  • Arms relaxed, a little away from your sides — not on your hips or crossed in front of you. We read your waist from the gap between your arms and your body.
  • Fitted clothing makes the biggest difference to accuracy. Loose or flowing clothes hide your shape — especially your waist — so we can't see it and the estimate is less accurate. We'll still measure the photo, and you can correct anything afterwards.
  • Even lighting, just you in the shot.

A phone propped up a few steps away, camera at about waist height, works better than a photo taken from above or at an angle.

We couldn't measure that photo

Your saved measurements haven't been changed. It's usually the framing or the pose — it happens often, and it's easy to fix.

  • Make sure your hips and legs are in the shot — step back from the camera if needed
  • Face the camera, standing straight, shoulders and hips level
  • Keep your arms a little away from your sides — not on your hips
  • Avoid sitting, posing, leaning or turning to the side
  • Even lighting, fitted clothing, just you in the shot

You can also skip this and type your measurements in yourself — it only takes a moment.
